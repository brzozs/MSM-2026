# Illustrative migration routes

The bird animation uses four species examples with named breeding and wintering regions. These are **hand-drawn illustrative corridors**, not measured tracks, forecasts of individual birds, exact stopover sites, or travel inferred from eBird sightings. The public ecology snapshot currently supplies sightings rather than measured departure/destination pairs. This catalog is display data and does not change any ecological calculation.

Each array in `web/src/data/migrationFlyways.ts` is ordered from seasonal departure to destination and contains geographic waypoints in `[longitude, latitude, display altitude]` order. Coordinates are representative anchors chosen for this map. Sources support the broad ranges and migration patterns below; they do not provide these coordinate sequences. Smoothing between waypoints is also a display choice. Altitudes of 480–600 km are exaggerated to clear the national state blocks and do not represent birds' flight heights. Animation speed and flock size do not represent measured movement or abundance.

## Source facts and display choices

| Species | Source-supported facts | Hand-chosen display anchors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Northern pintail | The [US Forest Service species review](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/anac) describes breeding in Alaska/Canada and migration to winter grounds including California. The [Cornell range account](https://www.birds.cornell.edu/landtrust/northern-pintail-nopi/) also describes the broad northern breeding and southern winter ranges. | Alaska → western Canada → Pacific Northwest → California Central Valley. This is one representative western corridor, not the route of every pintail. Spring reverses this illustrative corridor. |
| Sandhill crane | [USFWS sandhill crane surveys](https://www.fws.gov/project/sandhill-crane-surveys) describe the Mid-Continent Population's winter areas in Mexico and the southwestern U.S. and spring staging before entry into Canada. [Colorado Parks and Wildlife](https://cpw.state.co.us/species/sandhill-crane) describes its Canada/Alaska breeding range, southern winter areas, and central-plains migration corridor. | Northwest Canada → Saskatchewan → North Dakota → Nebraska's Platte River region → Texas Panhandle → northern Mexico. Spring reverses this representative corridor; other crane populations and resident subspecies are not modeled. |
| Swainson's hawk | [Texas Parks and Wildlife](https://tpwd.texas.gov/huntwild/wild/species/swainson/) describes western U.S./Canadian nesting and overland migration through Mexico and Central America to Argentina. [Cornell's range account](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Swainsons_Hawk/maps-range) identifies Texas, Mexico, and Central America as migration passage regions. | Western U.S. → Texas → eastern Mexico → Central America → western South America → Argentine Pampas. Intermediate coordinates are illustrative, including the South American leg. Spring reverses this broad corridor. |
| Blackpoll warbler | [Cornell's species account](https://www.birds.cornell.edu/landtrust/blackpoll-warbler-blpw/) describes a fall Atlantic crossing from the Eastern Seaboard and a spring return with Caribbean stops. [Covino et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.5751/ACE-01577-150117) discusses spring arrivals in Florida from the Antilles/South America and differing later pathways. [Audubon's account of loop migration](https://www.audubon.org/magazine/ask-kenn-do-migrating-birds-take-same-routes-spring-and-fall) describes the Caribbean/Florida spring route and offshore Atlantic fall route. | Fall: northeastern Canadian boreal forest → Atlantic Canada/New England → Atlantic Ocean → northern South America. Spring: northern South America → Caribbean → Florida → eastern U.S. → northeastern Canada. The two seasons deliberately use different paths. Ocean waypoints do not imply stops. |

## Seasonal and geographic limits

The application convention selects spring routes in March–May and fall routes in September–November, and withholds an inferred seasonal route in other months. These shared month windows are a display convention, not species-specific migration phenology. Species, populations, individuals, weather, and years differ; some actual movement falls outside these windows.

The routes retain their geographic endpoints when a state is selected. A focused state may show only the passing part of a corridor. A missing or offscreen corridor is not evidence that birds are absent. Bird sightings, measured radar density where supplied by the pipeline, and this illustrative movement remain separate concepts. No source map, photograph, or individual track dataset is copied into the catalog.

Source descriptions checked November 8, 2026. The blackpoll spring research paper is also available from the [Lesser Slave Lake Bird Observatory](https://www.lslbo.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Covino-et-al.-2020-Spring-migration-of-Blackpoll-Warblers-across-North-America.pdf).
